When to hire, and when exclusion is enough

Occupied attics and chimneys, nursing young, bats, and rabies-vector species are jobs for a licensed operator. DIY products on this site are for sealing after vacancy.

How to hire

Need a licensed operator

Hire from the official state lists and confirm the license is current with the issuing agency. This site is not an emergency service and does not dispatch operators.

How to use the lists

  • Ohio, New York, and Massachusetts require a licensed commercial operator for paid wildlife work. Doing that work for hire without the right license is a state-law problem, not a gray area.
  • City is the address of record on the source list. Some licensees live in a neighboring state and are licensed to work in Ohio or New York.
  • Ohio’s PDF is the operators who asked ODNR to publish their contact information. ODNR says it is not an exhaustive roster of every CNWACO permit. Always verify on the current PDF.
  • New York’s open-data file is the currently licensed commercial NWCO list. Municipality licenses are marked. Always verify on data.ny.gov.
  • This index does not scrape WildlifeHelp.org, CSIA, or private membership directories.
